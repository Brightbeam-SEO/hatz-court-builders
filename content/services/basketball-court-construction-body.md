## Common Basketball Court Problems That Start Beneath the Surface

A basketball court may look ready for play once the lines are painted, but its long-term condition depends heavily on the work completed underneath the finished surface.

Poor ground preparation, unsuitable materials, or a layout that doesn't fit the property can create problems after installation.

Some common basketball court construction concerns include:

- **Unstable ground:** Soil movement or poorly prepared areas can contribute to uneven pavement and surface damage.
- **Water collecting on the court:** Improper grading or drainage can leave puddles that interrupt play and affect court materials.
- **Cracked playing surfaces:** Existing concrete or asphalt may develop cracks from ground movement, weather exposure, or age.
- **Inconsistent ball response:** Uneven surfaces and unsuitable flooring can affect how the basketball bounces during games.
- **Restricted playing room:** A court may fit within a backyard but leave too little space beyond the sidelines or baselines.
- **Worn court markings:** Outdoor exposure and repeated use can cause lines and coatings to fade.
- **Missing playing features:** Fencing, lighting, hoop placement, and other components need to be considered during the layout stage.
- **Improper basketball hoop placement:** Equipment installed too close to obstacles or without suitable structural support can affect court functionality.
- **Inadequate surface traction:** Worn or unsuitable flooring may affect footing during quick stops and changes in direction.
- **Temperature-related deterioration:** Idaho's freezing winters and Arizona's extreme summer temperatures can influence court materials and foundation performance.
- **Insufficient overhead clearance:** Indoor basketball facilities need appropriate ceiling heights and equipment clearances.
- **Incorrect court markings:** Inaccurate free-throw lines, three-point arcs, and boundary dimensions can affect organized play and training.

## Basketball Court Foundations: What Goes Beneath the Playing Surface?

The foundation supports every part of a basketball court, including the finished surface, athletic equipment, and regular player movement.

A high-quality surface cannot independently correct problems caused by unstable ground or poorly prepared construction materials.

### Site Evaluation and Soil Preparation

Before construction, the proposed court area should be evaluated for soil conditions, existing slopes, drainage patterns, and previous construction activity.

Some Idaho properties may have moisture-sensitive soil, while Arizona properties may contain hard caliche, rocky terrain, or compacted construction fill.

Depending on the site, additional soil testing or engineering may be appropriate.

### Excavation and Grading

Existing vegetation, organic material, deteriorated pavement, and unsuitable soil may need to be removed.

Grading establishes the necessary elevations for the court and its surrounding drainage arrangements.

Outdoor basketball courts generally require a consistent playing plane with an appropriate drainage slope rather than an entirely level surface that retains water.

### Compacted Aggregate Base

A suitable aggregate base can help establish consistent support beneath concrete or asphalt court foundations.

The required material, depth, and compaction depend on the underlying soil, construction system, and expected loads.

Inadequate base preparation may contribute to uneven settlement, pavement movement, or recurring court damage.

### Concrete or Asphalt Foundation Installation

Once the site is appropriately prepared, a concrete or asphalt foundation can be installed according to the selected court system.

Foundation specifications should reflect the intended athletic use, site conditions, drainage requirements, and finished surface material.

### Preparing the Base for Athletic Surfacing

The supporting pavement must meet applicable requirements for surface condition, curing, moisture, elevations, and material compatibility before athletic coatings or modular flooring are installed.

Proper preparation supports a more consistent playing surface and helps reduce avoidable installation problems.

## Basketball Court Drainage and Surface Slope Requirements

### Establishing an Appropriate Playing Surface Slope

Outdoor basketball hardcourts generally require a slight, consistent slope to direct water toward suitable drainage areas.

The final slope should reflect the intended athletic use and project-specific construction requirements.

### Managing Runoff From Nearby Areas

Rainwater from roofs, patios, lawns, driveways, and surrounding landscapes should not flow uncontrollably onto the playing surface.

Perimeter grading and suitable drainage features can help redirect water away from the court.

### Protecting the Structural Base

Water accumulating beneath or around the court may affect certain soil and foundation conditions.

### Drainage Beneath Modular Basketball Flooring

Open-grid modular flooring allows water to pass through its tile openings.

Modular tiles cannot independently correct an inadequately drained concrete slab.

Drainage planning should be incorporated into the overall basketball court design rather than addressed only after surface installation.
