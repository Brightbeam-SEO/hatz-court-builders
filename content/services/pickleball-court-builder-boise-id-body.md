## Boise Pickleball Court Problems That Start With Poor Planning

A pickleball court needs a stable playing surface, enough room for movement, and markings that are easy to follow. Boise's changing seasons also affect how outdoor courts should be planned and maintained.

Here are some issues property owners may encounter:

- Winter moisture and freezing temperatures: Snow, ice, and freeze-thaw cycles can contribute to surface wear, especially when water enters existing cracks.
- Uneven ground: Sloped yards or undeveloped land may require grading before a court base can be constructed.
- Cracked pavement: Older concrete or asphalt surfaces may need repairs before receiving a new playing surface.
- Insufficient playing space: A court may fit within the available area, but players still need space beyond the boundary lines.
- Faded court markings: Regular use and outdoor exposure can leave lines difficult to see.
- Worn recreational surfaces: Courts used frequently by schools, neighborhoods, and parks may need resurfacing or other improvements.
