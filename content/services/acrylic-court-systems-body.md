## What Acrylic Court Systems Are — and Who They’re For

Acrylic systems are layered color and texture coatings applied over a prepared hard base. They are popular with homeowners building backyard courts, HOAs refreshing amenity courts, schools maintaining athletic pads, and clubs that need consistent play and a clean, branded look.

Light local climate pressure matters: Arizona UV and heat fade unprotected surfaces faster, while Idaho moisture and freeze cycles punish coatings applied over cracked or poorly drained pavement. Proper prep and product choice keep acrylic courts looking and playing better longer.

## When Acrylic Work Should Not Wait

Faded lines, peeling color coats, polished slick spots, and widespread surface chalking are more than cosmetic. They reduce traction, confuse multi-sport striping, and can lead to player injuries or closed courts.

If your facility has a tournament, seasonal opening, or HOA deadline approaching, acrylic court systems can restore playability faster than a full rebuild — provided the base is structurally sound. When the pavement is failing, we recommend repairing or replacing the base first.

## Common Problems Acrylic Court Systems Fix

Acrylic resurfacing and coating projects commonly address:

- Faded or chalky color coats
- Worn texture that feels slick underfoot
- Peeling or bubbling surface layers
- Hard-to-read or incorrect sport lines
- Patchy repairs that never blended with the original court
- Courts that look tired even though the base is still solid

These are the day-to-day pain points that drive searches for acrylic court systems — not vague “maintenance” issues.

## Why Professional Acrylic Installation Matters

Acrylic applied over dirty, wet, or unstable pavement fails early. Wrong texture changes ball bounce and footing. DIY kits rarely match commercial system thickness or curing standards.

A professional acrylic court system protects the base you already paid for and delivers the look and play quality players expect. Local install experience also helps with scheduling around weather windows in Idaho and Arizona.
