## Asphalt Court Bases for Outdoor Tennis, Pickleball & Basketball

Asphalt is a strong fit when you need a new outdoor court pad, a rebuild of a failing pavement surface, or a base designed specifically for sport coatings. It is commonly used for backyard courts, school athletic pads, HOA recreation areas, park courts, and commercial multi-sport facilities.

Idaho freeze-thaw cycles and Arizona heat both put stress on outdoor courts. Proper asphalt thickness, compaction, and slope help the court shed water and stay flatter longer — especially important before any color coating or cushion system goes down.

## Signs Your Court Needs New Asphalt Pavement

Some projects cannot wait on a cosmetic refresh. Standing water after storms, widespread alligator cracking, soft spots underfoot, or a court that has heaved enough to create trip hazards usually point to base failure. In those cases, overlaying new paint alone will not fix the problem.

If your court is unsafe, closed for play, or holding water across large sections, asphalt court installation (or a full rebuild) restores a sound foundation so coatings and lines can last. We prioritize site assessment so you know whether repair, resurfacing, or new pavement is the right path.

## Drainage, Cracking & Soft Spots Asphalt Courts Fix

Property owners in our Idaho and Arizona service areas often call when they see:

- Ponding water and poor court drainage
- Map cracking or alligator cracking across the playing area
- Soft or uneven spots that affect footing and ball bounce
- An old pad that was never sloped correctly for sports use
- Coatings that peel because the pavement underneath is failing
- A gravel or dirt recreation area that needs a permanent paved court

These issues are surface- and base-specific — not generic landscaping problems — and they show up quickly under regular athletic use.

A typical asphalt court installation project may include:

- Asphalt paving shaped for sport-court drainage

Some clients start with asphalt alone and schedule coatings later. Others complete the full system in one project timeline.

## Why Sport-Specific Asphalt Beats Generic Driveway Paving

DIY asphalt or a general paving crew that treats a court like a driveway often misses sport-specific slope, thickness, and finish tolerances. Incorrect pitch traps water. Thin pavement fails early. Rough texture makes coatings harder to apply evenly.

Professional asphalt court installation protects your investment in coatings, striping, and equipment. Local experience with Boise-area soils and Scottsdale heat also matters — climate and drainage patterns change how a court should be built and maintained.
