## Building Athletic Courts That Account for Phoenix's Desert Environment

Outdoor court construction in Phoenix requires careful consideration of the desert climate. Extreme summer temperatures, extended sunlight exposure, dust, and seasonal monsoon storms can affect athletic surfaces and supporting foundations.

Addressing these conditions during construction can help prevent avoidable performance and maintenance problems.

### Heat Exposure and Thermal Movement

Outdoor concrete, asphalt, and athletic flooring systems expand and contract as temperatures change.

Foundation design, pavement joints, material compatibility, and product-specific expansion requirements are important considerations when building courts in Phoenix.

Surface colors and sunlight exposure can also influence how warm a court becomes during the day.

### UV Exposure and Surface Deterioration

Prolonged exposure to strong sunlight can contribute to fading and deterioration of outdoor court coatings.

Selecting materials intended for exterior athletic use and following appropriate maintenance recommendations can help manage the effects of desert conditions.

### Desert Soil and Foundation Stability

Phoenix properties may contain different soil conditions, including clay-rich soils, disturbed fill, and compacted ground.

Some sites require additional excavation, grading, or soil preparation before a suitable court foundation can be installed.

Foundation requirements should be determined by the property's actual conditions rather than applying the same construction specifications to every installation.

### Monsoon Drainage and Water Management

Although Phoenix experiences relatively dry conditions throughout much of the year, monsoon storms can bring intense rainfall over short periods.

Courts require appropriate surface drainage and surrounding grading to help prevent standing water, erosion, and runoff problems.

Existing landscaping, irrigation systems, neighboring properties, and drainage easements should also be considered.

## Why the Court Foundation Matters as Much as the Finished Surface

A new athletic coating cannot compensate for an unstable or poorly prepared foundation.

The supporting structure helps establish the court's dimensions, surface consistency, drainage characteristics, and suitability for the selected athletic flooring.

Depending on the property, foundation construction may involve:

- Clearing vegetation or removing unsuitable existing materials.
- Excavating and grading the proposed playing area.
- Preparing and compacting the supporting aggregate.
- Establishing appropriate drainage conditions.
- Constructing a suitable concrete or asphalt base.
- Allowing applicable curing or preparation periods before athletic surface installation.

Concrete and asphalt can both support outdoor athletic surfaces when designed and constructed appropriately.

Concrete provides a rigid foundation, while asphalt offers a more flexible pavement structure. Neither material eliminates the need for appropriate soil preparation, drainage, or ongoing care.

The construction approach should account for the site, anticipated use, selected flooring system, and applicable structural requirements.

## Restoring an Existing Phoenix Court Without Unnecessary Reconstruction

An aging athletic court does not always require complete replacement.

| --- | --- |
| Faded court colors | Surface renewal and updated coatings |
| Worn or outdated playing lines | Court restriping |
| Repairable surface cracks | Applicable crack repairs followed by resurfacing |

## Protecting Your Athletic Court From Everyday Desert Conditions

Regular care can help maintain the appearance and playing characteristics of outdoor athletic surfaces.

Phoenix property owners should pay particular attention to dust accumulation, prolonged sunlight exposure, drainage, and seasonal storm debris.

General maintenance recommendations include:

- Remove loose dust, leaves, and debris using suitable cleaning methods.
- Inspect the court following heavy monsoon rainfall.
- Keep surrounding drainage areas free from obstructions.
- Monitor developing pavement cracks and surface deterioration.
- Avoid cleaning chemicals that are incompatible with the installed flooring.
- Minimize unnecessary irrigation overspray onto the court.
- Periodically inspect basketball hoops, net posts, fencing, and other equipment.
- Follow manufacturer recommendations for cleaning and maintaining athletic surface systems.

Maintenance requirements vary between acrylic coatings, modular flooring, synthetic turf, and other athletic materials.

Identifying surface or foundation problems early can help determine whether repairs or resurfacing are appropriate before deterioration becomes more extensive.
